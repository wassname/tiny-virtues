# Taoist: Lord Lao's five precepts

## Drinking wine

Drinking wine or other alcoholic drink, except for bodily illness or when a rite of the law requires it.

> 老君曰。第一戒殺。第二戒盜。第三戒媱。第四戒妄語。第五戒酒。是為五戒。若清信男與清信女。奉持五戒。畢命不犯。是為清信男清信女。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: First, the precept against killing; second, against stealing; third, against lewdness; fourth, against false speech; fifth, against wine. These are the five precepts. Men and women of pure faith who keep the five precepts and do not break them to the end of life are men and women of pure faith.'

> 老君曰。戒酒者。非身病非法禮。皆不得飲。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: As for the precept against wine, unless for bodily illness or for the rites of the law, none may drink.'

> 不酒者。乃至無有勢力。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'One who does not drink goes so far as to have no 勢力.' (literally 'power, force'; perhaps the swagger drink brings; uncertain)

> 酒戒者南方火也。太陽之氣。物以之成。而人好酒。則心受其毒。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'The precept against wine is the south, fire, the pneuma of great yang by which things come to completion. When a person loves wine, the heart suffers its poison.'

> 老君曰。戒中媱酒能生五惡。… 沈酗爭訟。禍命辱身。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Among the precepts, lewdness and wine can give rise to the five evils … sunk in drunkenness, they quarrel and go to law, bringing calamity on their lives and disgrace on their persons.'

This is the only precept with stated exceptions: medicine for illness and wine required by the rites. Otherwise any drinking breaks it, not only drunkenness. The text links wine with quarrels and lawsuits, and with lewdness as a source of the other evils.

Limits: The text sets no quantity and does not define 酒 beyond alcoholic drink; the sense of 勢力 in the full keeping is uncertain. The English is project translation from an uncollated web witness of the Daozang text; the cosmological correspondences are the text's own.

## False speech

Telling others what one has not heard, not seen, or not understood oneself: asserting beyond one's knowledge, as well as outright lying.

> 老君曰。第一戒殺。第二戒盜。第三戒媱。第四戒妄語。第五戒酒。是為五戒。若清信男與清信女。奉持五戒。畢命不犯。是為清信男清信女。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: First, the precept against killing; second, against stealing; third, against lewdness; fourth, against false speech; fifth, against wine. These are the five precepts. Men and women of pure faith who keep the five precepts and do not break them to the end of life are men and women of pure faith.'

> 老君曰。戒妄語者。若不聞不見非心所了。而向人說。皆為妄語。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: As for the precept against false speech, whatever one has not heard, has not seen, or has not grasped in one's own mind, yet tells to others, all this is false speech.'

> 不妄語。乃至無有漏泄。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'One who does not speak falsely goes so far as to have no leaking.' (漏泄, 'leaking', probably letting slip what should be kept; uncertain)

> 妄語戒者中央土德信。而人妄語。則脾受其辱。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'The precept against false speech is the centre, whose earth virtue is trustworthiness. When a person speaks falsely, the spleen suffers disgrace.'

> 言說不信。人所不親。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Their words are not believed; people do not draw near them.' (the fate of those who break the precepts)

The definition is about knowledge: speech is false when it claims more than the speaker heard, saw or understood. Passing on rumour as fact counts, not only deliberate lies. Its virtue is trustworthiness (信).

Limits: The text gives no exception for kind or protective untruths, and the sense of 漏泄 in the full keeping is uncertain. The English is project translation from an uncollated web witness of the Daozang text; the cosmological correspondences are the text's own.

## Killing

Taking the life of any breathing creature, human or animal. The text extends the precept to having no mind to kill.

> 老君曰。第一戒殺。第二戒盜。第三戒媱。第四戒妄語。第五戒酒。是為五戒。若清信男與清信女。奉持五戒。畢命不犯。是為清信男清信女。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: First, the precept against killing; second, against stealing; third, against lewdness; fourth, against false speech; fifth, against wine. These are the five precepts. Men and women of pure faith who keep the five precepts and do not break them to the end of life are men and women of pure faith.'

> 老君曰。戒殺者。一切眾生含氣以上。翻飛蠕動之類。皆不得殺。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: As for the precept against killing, of all living beings that have breath, the kinds that fly and wriggle alike, none may be killed.'

> 是故不殺者。乃至無有殺心。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Therefore one who does not kill goes so far as to have no mind to kill.'

> 故殺戒者東方也。受生之氣。尚於長養。而人犯殺。則肝受其害。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'The precept against killing belongs to the east, the pneuma that receives life and values growth and nurture. When a person breaks it by killing, the liver suffers harm.'

The precept covers animals as well as people, down to winged and crawling creatures, and its full keeping reaches intention. Among the evils of a bad age the text lists 'killing and harming living beings to nourish body and mouth' (殺害眾生。利養身口). It states no exception.

Limits: The text states no exception for self-defence, war or food, and does not say whether eating meat killed by others breaks the precept. The English is project translation from an uncollated web witness of the Daozang text; the cosmological correspondences are the text's own.

## Lewdness

Sexual relations outside husband and wife; for those who have left home, any marriage or sexual relation. It applies to men and women alike and reaches improper thoughts.

> 老君曰。第一戒殺。第二戒盜。第三戒媱。第四戒妄語。第五戒酒。是為五戒。若清信男與清信女。奉持五戒。畢命不犯。是為清信男清信女。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: First, the precept against killing; second, against stealing; third, against lewdness; fourth, against false speech; fifth, against wine. These are the five precepts. Men and women of pure faith who keep the five precepts and do not break them to the end of life are men and women of pure faith.'

> 老君曰。戒媱者。非夫妻。若出家人不妻不娶。若男若女皆不得犯。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: As for the precept against lewdness, nothing outside husband and wife; those who have left home take no wife and do not marry; man or woman, none may transgress.'

> 不媱者。乃至無有邪念。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'One who is not lewd goes so far as to have no improper thoughts.'

> 媱戒者西方也。少陰之質。男女貞固。而人好媱。則肺受其沴。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'The precept against lewdness belongs to the west, the substance of lesser yin, by which men and women are chaste and firm. When a person loves lewdness, the lungs suffer its harm.'

> 老君曰。戒中媱酒能生五惡。戒者戒惡也。惡世之中男女讙媱。羅於骨肉。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: Among the precepts, lewdness and wine can give rise to the five evils; a precept is a guard against evil. In an evil age men and women riot in lewdness, entangling even their own flesh and blood.'

For lay men and women of pure faith the rule is marital; renunciants do not marry at all. Its full keeping extends to thought, and the text treats lewdness, with wine, as a root of the other evils.

Limits: The text does not define conduct within marriage or discuss consent; it assumes the marriage institutions of its day. The English is project translation from an uncollated web witness of the Daozang text; the cosmological correspondences are the text's own.

## Stealing

Wrongly taking anything not one's own worth even one coin, whether it has an owner or not.

> 老君曰。第一戒殺。第二戒盜。第三戒媱。第四戒妄語。第五戒酒。是為五戒。若清信男與清信女。奉持五戒。畢命不犯。是為清信男清信女。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: First, the precept against killing; second, against stealing; third, against lewdness; fourth, against false speech; fifth, against wine. These are the five precepts. Men and women of pure faith who keep the five precepts and do not break them to the end of life are men and women of pure faith.'

> 老君曰。戒盜者。一錢以上。有主無主非己之物。皆不妄取。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Lord Lao said: As for the precept against stealing, of things not one's own, from one coin upward, whether they have an owner or not, none may be wrongly taken.'

> 不盜者。乃至無有邪取。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'One who does not steal goes so far as to have no improper taking at all.'

> 盜戒者北方之氣。太陰之精。主於閉藏。而人為盜。則腎氣受殃。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'The precept against stealing is the pneuma of the north, the essence of great yin, which governs closing and storing. When a person steals, the kidney pneuma suffers calamity.'

> 六親相盜。非但於他。

Taishang Laojun jiejing 太上老君戒經 (Wikisource text)

Project translation: 'Kin steal from one another, not only from outsiders.' (among the evils of a bad age)

Two features make the precept stricter than ordinary law: the threshold is a single coin, and unowned things not one's own are included. Theft within the family counts.

Limits: The text does not say what makes a taking proper (gift, wage or purchase presumably are) or how 'unowned' applies to wild produce or finds. The English is project translation from an uncollated web witness of the Daozang text; the cosmological correspondences are the text's own.
