# Taoist: Zhuangzi's four evils (四患, ch. 31)

## Avarice

Zhuangzi 31 four evils (Watson tr.): appropriating others’ affairs, not merely money.

> … To insist that you know it all, that everything be done your way, snatching from others and appropriating for your own use—this is called avarice. …

Zhuangzi 31 The Old Fisherman, trans. Burton Watson 2013, stranger speaking

The fault overlaps with officiousness, but here the marker is controlling and appropriating.

## Avidity

Zhuangzi 31 four evils besetting undertakings (Watson tr.), with the stranger’s role critique.

> “As for the four evils that I spoke of, to be fond of plunging into great undertakings, altering and departing from the old accepted ways, hoping thereby to enhance your merit and fame—this is called avidity. … If you do away with the eight faults and avoid committing the four evils, then and only then will you become capable of being taught!”

Zhuangzi 31 The Old Fisherman, trans. Burton Watson 2013, stranger speaking

Not all change is avidity. The fame aim is part of the definition.

## Bigotry

Zhuangzi 31 four evils (Watson tr.): agreement-based evaluation.

> … When men agree with you, to commend them; when they disagree with you, to refuse to see any goodness in them even when it is there—this is called bigotry. …

Zhuangzi 31 The Old Fisherman, trans. Burton Watson 2013, stranger speaking

The fault is not limited to religious hostility. It requires that goodness actually be present and be denied because of disagreement.

## Obstinacy

Zhuangzi 31 four evils (Watson tr.), and Confucius’s response in the chapter.

> … To see your errors but refuse to change, to listen to remonstrance but go on behaving worse than before—this is called obstinacy. …

Zhuangzi 31 The Old Fisherman, trans. Burton Watson 2013, stranger speaking

> … “I am aware of no error of my own, and yet why did I fall victim to these four persecutions?” … Confucius once more bowed twice, straightened up, and said, … “I would like to be allowed to go there, receive instruction, and at last learn the Great Way!”

Zhuangzi 31 The Old Fisherman, trans. Burton Watson 2013, stranger speaking

Disagreeing with a remonstrance one judges mistaken is not shown by the source to be obstinacy. The source leaves this case open.
