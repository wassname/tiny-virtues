# Taoist: Laozi's four self-displays (ch. 24)

## Asserting his own views

Laozi 24's second fault, with ch. 22's freedom from self-assertion.

> (So), he who displays himself does not shine; he who asserts his own views is not distinguished; … Hence those who pursue (the course) of the Tao do not adopt and allow them.

Laozi, Daodejing 24, trans. James Legge(1891)

> He is free from self-display, and therefore he shines; from self-assertion, and therefore he is distinguished; …

Laozi, Daodejing 22, trans. James Legge(1891)

Limits: This does not ban stating views. The boundary between stating a view and asserting it is undecided in the source.

## Displaying himself

Laozi 24's first self-display fault, with ch. 22's freedom from self-display.

> 24. He who stands on his tiptoes does not stand firm; … (So), he who displays himself does not shine; … Such conditions, viewed from the standpoint of the Tao, are like remnants of food, or a tumour on the body, which all dislike.

Laozi, Daodejing 24, trans. James Legge(1891)

> Therefore the sage holds in his embrace the one thing (of humility), and manifests it to all the world. He is free from self-display, and therefore he shines; …

Laozi, Daodejing 22, trans. James Legge(1891)

Limits: The four faults overlap in ch. 24, so they are not exclusive types. Visibility imposed by a duty is not decided by the text.

## Self-conceit

Laozi 24's fourth fault, with ch. 22's freedom from self-complacency.

> … he who is self-conceited has no superiority allowed to him. Such conditions, viewed from the standpoint of the Tao, are like remnants of food, or a tumour on the body, which all dislike.

Laozi, Daodejing 24, trans. James Legge(1891)

> He is free from self-display, and therefore he shines; … from self-complacency, and therefore he acquires superiority. It is because he is thus free from striving that therefore no one in the world is able to strive with him.

Laozi, Daodejing 22, trans. James Legge(1891)

Limits: Legge uses 'self-conceited' in ch. 24 and 'self-complacency' in ch. 22. Accurate self-knowledge is not decided by the text.

## Vaunting himself

Laozi 24's third fault, with ch. 22's freedom from self-boasting.

> … he who vaunts himself does not find his merit acknowledged; … Hence those who pursue (the course) of the Tao do not adopt and allow them.

Laozi, Daodejing 24, trans. James Legge(1891)

> He is free from self-display, and therefore he shines; … from self-boasting, and therefore his merit is acknowledged; …

Laozi, Daodejing 22, trans. James Legge(1891)

Limits: This fault overlaps with self-display. Required reports or records are not judged by the text.
