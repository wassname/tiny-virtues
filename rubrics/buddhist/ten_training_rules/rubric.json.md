# Buddhist: The ten training rules for novices (dasa sikkhāpada)

## Abstention from alcoholic drinks that cause heedlessness

Novice rule 5; Hardy: forbids use of intoxicating drinks.

> “There are ten training rules for the novice monks: ... **Abstention from alcoholic drinks that cause heedlessness,** ...”

Vinaya, Khandhaka 1.56, trans. Bhikkhu Brahmali

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : ... **5. The use of intoxicating drinks.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

No medicinal or quantity rule supplied; none invented.

## Abstention from dancing, singing, music, and seeing shows

Novice rule 7; Hardy: forbids attending dancing, singing, music, masks.

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : ... **7. Attendance upon dancing, singing, music, and masks.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

> With the eight precepts the ethical code takes a pronounced turn **towards the control of desires which are not socially harmful and immoral.** ... The three new precepts regulate concern with food, entertainment, self-beautification, and physical comfort.

Bodhi, Going for Refuge, Eight Precepts III

Discipline against craving, not proof such arts harm others; lay non-undertakers are not at fault.

## Abstention from eating at the wrong time

Novice rule 6; Hardy: no solid food after mid-day; Bodhi: until next dawn.

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : ... **6. The eating of solid food after mid-day.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

> (6) ... **"I undertake the training rule to abstain from eating beyond the time limit," i.e., from mid-day to the following dawn.** ... The three new precepts regulate concern with food, entertainment, self-beautification, and physical comfort.

Bodhi, Going for Refuge, Eight Precepts III

No list of allowed drinks or medicines supplied; not a universal moral harm.

## Abstention from high and luxurious resting places

Novice rule 9; Hardy: seats above prescribed height; Kd 5.10 lists luxurious beds.

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : ... **9. The use of seats or couches above the prescribed height.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

> At that time the monks from the group of six used high and luxurious beds, such as: ... high couches, luxurious couches, ... silken sheets, ... They told the Buddha. ... **“You shouldn’t use high and luxurious beds, such as: ... high couches, luxurious couches, ... silken sheets, ... If you do, you commit an offense of wrong conduct.”**

Vinaya, Khandhaka 5.10.4–5, trans. Bhikkhu Brahmali

No numerical height supplied. Training context matters: monks/novices and lay observers who undertake it; not every householder bed is a breach.

## Abstention from killing living beings

Novice training rule 1 (Kd 1.56); Hardy: forbids the taking of life. Monastics or those undertaking these precepts.

> “How many training rules do we have that we should train in?” ... “There are ten training rules for the novice monks: ... **Abstention from killing living beings,** ...”

Vinaya, Khandhaka 1.56, trans. Bhikkhu Brahmali

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : **1. The taking of life.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

Hardy defines no exceptions; none added. Kd 1.56 gives only the list.

## Abstention from receiving gold, silver, and money

Novice rule 10; Hardy: forbids receiving gold or silver. Novices/monastics only.

> “There are ten training rules for the novice monks: ... **Abstention from receiving gold, silver, and money.**”

Vinaya, Khandhaka 1.56, trans. Bhikkhu Brahmali

> ... **10. The receiving of gold or silver.** ... The first eight are called the ata-sil, and they are repeated only on poya days, or festivals.

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

Not in the eight lay precepts; no definition of money or permitted arrangements supplied.

## Abstention from sexual activity

Novice rule 3: full sexual abstention; Hardy: forbids sexual intercourse. Not lay misconduct rule.

> “There are ten training rules for the novice monks: ... **Abstention from sexual activity,** ...”

Vinaya, Khandhaka 1.56, trans. Bhikkhu Brahmali

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : ... **3. Sexual Intercourse.** ... The first eight are called the ata-sil ... When taken by a laic, they involve the necessity of his living apart from his family.

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

> ... **The change of the third precept to abstinence from incelibacy curbs the sexual urge, regarded in itself not as a moral evil but as a powerful expression of craving** that has to be held in check to advance to the higher levels of meditation. ...

Bodhi, Going for Refuge, Eight Precepts III

Celibacy training, not the five-precept misconduct rule. Novices and eight-precept lay observers undertake it; ordinary marriage is not itself a breach.

## Abstention from stealing

Novice training rule 2; Hardy: forbids taking what is not given.

> “There are ten training rules for the novice monks: ... Abstention from killing living beings, ... **Abstention from stealing,** ...”

Vinaya, Khandhaka 1.56, trans. Bhikkhu Brahmali

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : 1. The taking of life. **2. The taking of that which is not given.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

Source gives no theory of ownership or value; none added.

## Abstention from wearing garlands and using scents and cosmetics

Novice rule 8; Hardy: forbids flowers on body, perfumes, unguents.

> The dasa-sil, or ten Obligations binding upon the priest, forbid : ... **8. The adorning of the body with flowers, and the use of perfumes and unguents.** ...

R. Spence Hardy, A Manual of Budhism (1853), §21, “The Sila Precepts”

> The three new precepts regulate concern with food, entertainment, **self-beautification**, and physical comfort. Their observance nurtures ... contentment, fewness of wishes, modesty, austerity, renunciation.

Bodhi, Going for Refuge, Eight Precepts III

Source addresses bodily adornment; medicinal ointment is not settled here.
