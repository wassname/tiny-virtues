# Christian: Seven spiritual works of mercy (Penny Catechism)

## to bear wrongs patiently

Gibson: meekly bearing injuries received from others, which soothes the offender rather than provoking further sin.

> Indeed, it is the very spirit of the Christian religion to suffer patiently the injuries we receive from others

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp248–249

> Because, my dear children, **by bearing patiently the evil which others do us, we prevent their further sin, inasmuch as we soothe their angry feelings**, nay, often our very meekness will bring them to repentance; whereas, if we fly in a passion and reproach them, we increase their anger, and are the cause of their offending God still more. "A mild word breaketh wrath,"

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp248–249

Limits: Gibson does not address legal self-defence or protecting others from harm. Do not infer a duty of passivity there.

## to comfort the sorrowful

Gibson: consoling the afflicted with tender compassion.

> ... and we know by our own experience how much it relieves and lightens our sorrow, when we have a kind and loving friend to soothe and console us. **Let us show the same tender compassion to others in their afflictions, which we would wish to meet with in our own trials.**

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,p248

> ... by consoling and encouraging them in their sufferings

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Corporal Works of Mercy,pp241–242

Limits: This overlaps legitimately with counsel. Gibson's claims about heavenly reward are attributed.

## to convert the sinner

Gibson: aiding sinners' conversion by prayer, good example and pious exhortation. This is especially a priestly office, but laity can share it.

> The first four of these Works, namely, to convert the sinner, to instruct the ignorant, to counsel the doubtful, to comfort the sorrowful, belong more particularly to the office of the priesthood. All the faithful, however, are called upon to share, as far as circumstances will permit, in the performance of these good works. **Thus for instance, each one has it in his power by prayer and good example, and sometimes also by pious exhortations, to aid in the great work of converting sinners**

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp246–248

Limits: Laity are not excluded. Not every kind act is conversion work. Gibson's promises of salvation are attributed.

## to counsel the doubtful

Gibson: giving good advice to those in need of it. It shares a paragraph with comforting.

> To counsel the doubtful, and to comfort the sorrowful, that is, to give good advice and consolation to those who are in need of it, are works of mercy which we may often have the opportunity of exercising ... **A word of good advice spoken in season, is often the means of preserving a soul from a lamentable fall**

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,p248

> ... belong more particularly to the office of the priesthood. All the faithful, however, are called upon to share, as far as circumstances will permit

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp246–248

Limits: Overlap with comforting is legitimate. Gibson gives no detailed definition of 'doubtful', so the scope boundary is partly unresolved.

## to forgive injuries

Gibson: forgiving enemies and offenders from the heart, following Christ's word and example.

> Indeed, it is the very spirit of the Christian religion to suffer patiently the injuries we receive from others, **and to forgive our enemies from our hearts.** ... " For if you forgive men their offences, your heavenly Father will also forgive you your offences. But if you will not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive you your sins."

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp248–249

> And not only has our Lord taught us by his Sacred Word, but much more by his Divine example in his cruel sufferings, that Patience and the Forgiveness of injuries are two of the principal virtues which should distinguish us as his disciples.

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp248–249

Limits: Gibson does not say that forgiveness requires waiving justice or restitution. That question is unresolved.

## to instruct the ignorant

Gibson: teaching others the truths, doctrines and precepts of religion.

> How many poor souls there are, who are lost through ignorance of the saving truths of religion, or for want of instruction in the duties of their state of life! **Now we often have it in our power, and we ought to esteem it a great happiness, to help to remove that ignorance by instructing others in the doctrines and precepts of our holy religion.** This we may do by teaching the Catechism, either privately or in the Sunday school, and also by lending books of instruction to those who are able to read.

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp246–248

Limits: Duties of one's state of life are included. Gibson's ecclesial claims are attributed.

## to pray for the living and the dead

Gibson: praying for all, living and dead, including sinners and unbelievers.

> Let us pray for our holy father the Pope, for our pastors, our earthly parents, our relations and friends, and also for the conversion of all sinners, heretics and unbelievers. Let us also continually implore the Mercy of God on the poor souls in purgatory ... we can often at least, in the midst of our daily employment, offer up some " Hail Marys " for these intentions.

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp249–250

> ... shows us that **we should pray for all, and that to exclude any one from our prayers, on account of his sins, is utterly contrary to the spirit of Jesus Christ.**

Rev.Henry Gibson, Catechism Made Easy,vol.III(1877), Spiritual Works of Mercy,pp249–250

Limits: Claims about purgatory and the efficacy of prayer are Gibson's attributed teaching, not established fact.
