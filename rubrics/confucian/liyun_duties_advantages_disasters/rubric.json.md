# Confucian: The things which men consider right (人義, ten duties), the things advantageous to men (人利) and the things disastrous to men (人患)

## Benevolence on the part of the ruler

The ruler's side of ruler–minister; Li Yun frames the sage ruler's task.

> He must know men’s feelings, lay open to them what they consider right, show clearly to them what is advantageous, and comprehend what are their calamities.

Li Ki, Book VII, Li Yun, section II §§18-19, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 379

> **with benevolence on the part of the ruler, and loyalty on that of the minister**; — these ten are the things which men consider to be right.

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

君仁 is named, not defined here.

## Cultivation of harmony

Second of Li Yun's two things advantageous to men; paired with putting away quarrelling.

> **Truthfulness in speech and the cultivation of harmony constitute what are called ‘ the things advantageous to men.’** ... promote truthfulness of speech, and the maintenance of harmony ; show his value for kindly consideration and complaisant courtesy; and put away quarrelling and plundering,

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

Term undefined; harmony is not mere silence or compliance.

## Deference on the part of juniors

The junior's side of the elder–junior pair in Li Yun's ten.

> kindness on the part of elders, **and deference on that of juniors**; ... — these ten are the things which men consider to be right. ... show his value for kindly consideration and complaisant courtesy;

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

幼順 is undefined; juniors relative to elders, kin or not.

Ordinary politeness to strangers is not automatically this role duty; seniority relation is needed.

## Filial duty on the part of the son

The son's side of the father–son pair; Xiaojing's wrong-command teaching is separately attributed.

> What are ‘ the things which men consider right ? ’ **Kindness on the part of the father, and filial duty on that of the son**;

Li Ki, Book VII, Li Yun, section II §§18-19, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 379

> — **these ten are the things which men consider to be right.**

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

> “When the command is wrong, a son should resist his father, and a minister should resist his August Master. “The maxim is, ‘Resist when wrongly commanded.’ **Hence how can he be called filial who obeys his father when he is commanded to do wrong?**”

Classic of Filial Piety, chapter XV, Ivan Chen, The Book of Filial Duty (1908)

Li Yun does not define the duty. Xiaojing is related teaching; Mencius 4B30 on reproof differs and is unresolved here.

## Gentleness on the part of the elder brother

The elder brother's side of the brothers' pair in Li Yun's ten.

> What are ‘ the things which men consider right ? ’ Kindness on the part of the father, and filial duty on that of the son; **gentleness on the part of the elder brother**

LiKi BookVII LiYun§19, Legge1885, pp379–380

兄良 is named, not explained.

Term only; no source narrative. Brother pair, not every senior.

## Kindness on the part of elders

The elder's side of the elder–junior pair; Li Yun states no kinship restriction.

> **kindness on the part of elders, and deference on that of juniors**; ... — these ten are the things which men consider to be right.

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

長惠 is named, not defined; elders may be nonkin seniors.

Term only. Age-seniority role, not automatic parenthood or teacher role.

## Kindness on the part of the father

Li Yun names the father's side of the father–son pair among ten things men consider right.

> What are ‘ the things which men consider right ? ’ **Kindness on the part of the father, and filial duty on that of the son**;

LiKi BookVII LiYun§19, Legge1885, pp379–380

Li Yun names 父慈 without defining it.

Li Yun gives the term only; no definition or narrative. Not a duty of teachers or strangers.

## Loyalty on the part of the minister

The minister's side of ruler–minister; Xiaojing's resistance teaching is separately attributed.

> with benevolence on the part of the ruler, **and loyalty on that of the minister**; — these ten are the things which men consider to be right.

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

> **“When the command is wrong, a son should resist his father, and a minister should resist his August Master.**

Classic of Filial Piety, chapter XV, Ivan Chen, The Book of Filial Duty (1908)

Li Yun does not define loyalty; Xiaojing is related text, not Li Yun's words.

## Murders

Third of Li Yun's disasters; 相殺 literally 'killing one another'.

> **Quarrels, plundering, and murders are ‘ the things disastrous to men.’**

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

Li Yun does not address war, lawful punishment or accident; that gap is unresolved.

## Obedience on the part of the younger brother

The younger brother's side of the brothers' pair in Li Yun's ten.

> gentleness on the part of the elder brother, and obedience on that of ... the younger;

LiKi BookVII LiYun§19, Legge1885, pp379–380

弟弟 is undefined; Xiaojing's wrong-command teaching addresses sons and ministers, not brothers.

Li Yun gives no rule on wrong commands for brothers; that gap is unresolved here.

## Plundering

Second of Li Yun's three things disastrous to men.

> **Quarrels, plundering, and murders are ‘ the things disastrous to men.’** ... and put away quarrelling and plundering,

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

奪 is named, not defined; no source narrative.

## Quarrels

First of Li Yun's three things disastrous to men; a general list, not each duty's negation.

> **Quarrels, plundering, and murders are ‘ the things disastrous to men.’** Hence, when a sage (ruler) would ... put away quarrelling and plundering, if he neglect the rules of propriety, how shall he succeed ?

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

爭 is named, not defined. Calm disagreement or remonstrance is not automatically a quarrel.

## Righteousness on the part of the husband

The husband's side of Li Yun's historical husband–wife pair, attributed not endorsed.

> **righteousness on the part of the husband, and submission on that of the wife** ; ... — these ten are the things which men consider to be right.

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

夫義 is undefined; Safford's later text expects a superior husband to value true words.

Hierarchy is historical and attributed. Safford is a separate later text, not Li Yun's definition.

## Submission on the part of the wife

The wife's side of Li Yun's historical pair; attributed, not modernised or endorsed.

> righteousness on the part of the husband, **and submission on that of the wife** ; ... — these ten are the things which men consider to be right.

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

> A faithful minister, if his monarch does wrong reproves him ; **a virtuous wife, in the same way, tells her husband when he is in fault, and aids him to do better.** A really superior man does not desire flattery from his wife, but her true words are precious.

Typical Women of China, Woman’s WordschII, trSafford1891, p92

Historical hierarchy attributed only. Safford's reproof teaching is a later text, not Li Yun's own words.

## Truthfulness in speech

First of Li Yun's two things advantageous to men; general, not role-bound.

> **Truthfulness in speech and the cultivation of harmony constitute what are called ‘ the things advantageous to men.’** ... Hence, when a sage (ruler) would ... promote truthfulness of speech, and the maintenance of harmony ; ... if he neglect the rules of propriety, how shall he succeed ?

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

Li Yun names, not defines, 講信. Silence is not falsehood.
