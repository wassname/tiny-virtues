# Confucian: Five Relationships (五倫 wulun; Mencius' 'relations of humanity')

## Affection between father and son

Mencius 3A4 (Legge 1875): affection taught as a relation of humanity between actual father and son.

> ...he appointed Seeh to be minister of Instruction, and to teach the relations of humanity ! — how, **between father and son, there should be affection**; between ruler and subject, righteousness...

Mencius 3A4 §8, James Legge, The Works of Mencius (1875 revised edition), p. 209

> The ancients exchanged sons, and one taught the son of another. ... Between father and son there should be no reproving admonitions as to what is good. Such reproofs lead to alienation; and than alienation there is nothing more inauspicious.

Mencius4A18, tr. Legge1875, p247: teaching sons, anger and alienation

> "To urge one another by reproofs to what is good is the way of friends. **But such urging between father and son is the greatest injury to the kindly feeling [that should prevail between them].**"

Mencius4B30, tr. Legge1875, pp267–268: common list and Chang-tsze

Mencius warns that father-son reproof alienates. Xiaojing 15, a separate text, urges resisting wrong commands. These sources differ.

3A4 names affection without detailing duties. Xiaojing's counsel to resist wrong commands is separately attributed and in tension with 4A18. The role is actual father and son, not other pairs.

## Attention to separate functions between husband and wife

Mencius names spousal distinction; related Li Ki Nei Ze rites illustrate it without supplying Mencius's complete definition.

> ...**between husband and wife, attention to their separate functions**...

Mencius 3A4 §8, James Legge, The Works of Mencius (1875 revised edition), p. 209

> Males and females did not use the same stand or rack for their clothes. **The wife did not presume to hang up anything on the pegs or stand of her husband; nor to put anything in his boxes or satchels; nor to share his bathing-house.** When her husband had gone out (from their apartment), she put his pillow in its case, rolled up his upper and under mats, put them in their covers, and laid them away in their proper receptacles.

LiKi BookX, NeiZe sectionII §§13–15, tr. James Legge, SBE27(1885), pp470–471

> **As between husband and wife, it was not until they were seventy, that they deposited these things in the same place without separation.**

LiKi BookX, NeiZe sectionII §§13–15, tr. James Legge, SBE27(1885), pp470–471

Concrete examples apply the related elite-household rite, not Mencius's own gloss. Storage is shared at seventy; this sets no rule for other relations.

## Fidelity between friends

Mencius 3A4: fidelity (信) between actual friends; 4B30 on friendly reproof.

> ...**and between friends, fidelity.**

Mencius 3A4 §8, James Legge, The Works of Mencius (1875 revised edition), p. 209

> "**To urge one another by reproofs to what is good is the way of friends.**"

Mencius4B30, tr. Legge1875, pp267–268: common list and Chang-tsze

> "**If a man has a good friend to resist him in doing bad actions, he will have his reputation preserved**..."

Classic of Filial Piety, chapter XV, Ivan Chen, The Book of Filial Duty (1908)

Li Yun, a related text, counts truthfulness in speech among the things advantageous to men.

Mencius does not define 信 here. Overlap with reproof is drawn from 4B30 and Xiaojing and is not a complete account.

## Proper distinction between old and young

Mencius 3A4: proper order between elder and younger (長幼), with related Li Yun duties.

> ...**between old and young, a proper distinction**...

Mencius 3A4 §8, James Legge, The Works of Mencius (1875 revised edition), p. 209

> ... gentleness on the part of the elder brother, and obedience on that of ... the younger;

Li Ki, Book VII, Li Yun, section II §§18-19, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 379; Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

> ... kindness on the part of elders, and deference on that of juniors ... Quarrels, plundering, and murders are ‘the things disastrous to men.’

Li Ki, Li Yun, section II §§19-20, James Legge, SBE 27 (1885), p. 380

Li Yun, a related text, makes the order reciprocal: elders owe kindness and juniors owe deference.

3A4 leaves the distinction unexplained. The details come from Li Yun and are not Mencius's words. The item is not obedience between arbitrary strangers.

## Righteousness between ruler and subject

Mencius 3A4: righteousness between actual ruler and minister or subject; related Xiaojing and 3B2 context.

> ...to teach the relations of humanity ! — how, between father and son, there should be affection; **between ruler and subject, righteousness**...

Mencius 3A4 §8, James Legge, The Works of Mencius (1875 revised edition), p. 209

> When the command is wrong, a son should resist his father, and a minister should resist his August Master. ... The maxim is, ‘Resist when wrongly commanded.’

Classic of Filial Piety, chapter XV, Ivan Chen, The Book of Filial Duty (1908)

> ... when he obtains his desire [for office], ... to practise his principles for the good of the people; and when that desire is disappointed, to practise them alone; to be above the power of riches and honours to make dissipated, of poverty and mean condition to make swerve [from principle], and of power and force to make bend: — these characteristics constitute the great man.

Mencius3B2, tr. Legge1875, pp217–218: ritual-wife comparison and great-man teaching

Xiaojing and Li Yun (where ruler benevolence and minister loyalty are paired) are related teachings, not Mencius's words.

3A4 does not define righteousness in this relation. The details here come from separately attributed Xiaojing and 3B2 great-man context.
