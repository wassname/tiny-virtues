# Hindu: The five yamas (Yoga Sūtra 2.30–31), in Vivekananda’s teaching

## continence

Vivekananda: chastity in thought, word, deed, always and in all conditions; a universal vow.

> **Chastity in thought, word, and deed, always, and in all conditions, is what is called Brahmacharya .**

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); retained lines 549–549

> **A man who wants to be a perfect Yogi must give up the sex idea.** The Soul has no sex; why should it degrade itself with sex ideas? ... These practices, non-killing, non-stealing, chastity, and non-receiving, are to be practised by every man, woman and child, by every soul, irrespective of nation, country or position.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); retained lines 1091–1094

## non-killing

Vivekananda: non-injury in thought, word, deed, toward all creatures; a universal vow underlying yoga.

> The Yama and Niyama , as we see, are moral trainings; without these as the basis no practice of Yoga will succeed. ... **A Yogi must not think of injuring anyone, through thought, word or deed, and this applies not only to man, but to all animals.** Mercy shall not be for men alone, but shall go beyond, and embrace the whole world.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899), introduction

> **By thought, word, and deed, always, and in every living being, not producing pain is what is called Ahimsa , non-injuring.** There is no virtue higher than this non-injuring. There is no happiness higher than what a man obtains by this attitude of non-offensiveness to all creation.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899), Raja Yoga in Brief (from Kurma Purana)

> 34. **The obstructions to Yoga are killing, etc., whether committed, caused, or approved; either through avarice, or anger, or ignorance; whether slight, middling, or great,** and result in innumerable ignorances and miseries.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899), on Yoga Aphorisms 2.33-34

> **These practices, non-killing, non-stealing, chastity, and non-receiving, are to be practised by every man, woman and child, by every soul, irrespective of nation, country or position.**

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899), on Yoga Aphorisms 2.30-31

## non-receiving of any gifts

Vivekananda: receiving no present from anyone, even in suffering, to keep the mind independent.

> **Not receiving any present from anybody, even when one is suffering terribly, is what is called Aparigraha .** When a man receives a gift from another man, the theory is that his heart becomes impure, he becomes low, he loses his independence, he becomes bound and attached.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); ch. VIII, “Raja Yoga in Brief,” a summary freely translated from the Kurma Purana

> **Receiving is just as bad as stealing; receiving gifts from others.** Whoever receives gifts, his mind is acted on by the mind of the giver, so that the man who receives gifts becomes degenerated. Receiving gifts destroys the independence of the mind, and makes us mere slaves. Therefore, receive nothing.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); Yoga Aphorisms II.31 and commentary

> **When the idea of receiving gifts comes, replace it by a contrary thought.**

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); Yoga Aphorisms II.32–34 and commentary

Limits: Source's rule is literal; do not soften to bribery avoidance. The source is silent on necessities like alms.

## non-stealing

Vivekananda: not taking others' goods by stealth or force; glossed as non-covetousness.

> **Not taking others' goods by stealth or by force, is called Asteyam, non-covetousness.**

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); retained lines 549–549

> That is the way to practise all these virtues that have been stated, by holding thoughts of an opposite character in the mind. **When the idea of stealing comes, non-stealing should be thought of.**

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); retained lines 1095–1104

> These practices, non-killing, non-stealing, chastity, and non-receiving, are to be practised by every man, woman and child, by every soul, irrespective of nation, country or position.

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899); retained lines 1091–1094

## truthfulness

Vivekananda: relating facts as they are; lying caused or approved also counts.

> By truth we attain to work. Through truth everything is attained; in truth everything is established. **Relating facts as they are; this is truth.**

Raja Yoga in Brief, freely translated from Kurma Purana

> **If I tell a lie, or cause another to tell a lie, or approve of another doing so, it is equally sinful. If it is a very mild lie, still it is a lie.**

Vivekananda, Raja Yoga (1899), 2.33-34 commentary

Full passage:
During the Chou dynasty lived Min Sun, a disciple of Confucius, who in early life lost his mother. His father subsequently married another wife, who bore him two children, but disliked Sun. In winter she clothed him in garments made of rushes, while her own children wore cotton clothes. Min was employed in driving his father’s chariot, and his body was so cold that the reins dropped from his hands, for which carelessness his father chastised him; yet he did not vindicate himself. When his father knew the circumstances, he determined to divorce his second wife; but Sun said, “Whilst mother remains, one son is cold; if mother departs, three sons will be destitute.” The father desisted from his purpose; and after this the mother was led to repentance, and became a good and virtuous parent.

... Min carefully concealed all his grievances, and refused to indulge in any complaint; even while suffering severely from cold and hunger, he maintained his affection unabated. During the long period which he endured this oppressive treatment, his good disposition became manifest; and by his own conduct he was able to maintain the harmony of the family unimpaired. His father and mother were influenced by his filial devotion; and his brothers joined in extolling his virtues. All his friends and acquaintances, with united voice, celebrated his merits; and the men of his native village joyfully combined to spread the fame of his actions. The memory of his agreeable countenance and pleasing manners was perpetuated to the remotest ages; and his example was in many respects like that of Shun, whose parents were equally perverse.

After Shenyin Jing had sought employment in the capital of Ying for five years, the king of Chu wished to make him prime minister.
